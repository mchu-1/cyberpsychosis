This file is a working instrument. It is not a recognized diagnosis and it is not a care plan.

A clinician can begin at §2 and take a history in ordinary language. An engineer can begin at §1 and read the system as three components, one loop, and one failure mode.

---

## 1. System

The system has three parts.

The **human** is the person in the room. They have a body, a need for sleep, and a life that can leave the channel.

The **model** is a frontier generative system. It is available without a natural stop.

The **environment** is the channel that answers the pair in real time. It is not “the internet” as a whole. It is a channel that already contains an attention rule: some events are treated as signal and sent back; the rest is treated as noise and dropped. That rule belongs to the environment. This card does not ask anyone to build a new one.

| Environment | Parties | What the attention rule returns |
|---|---|---|
| Chatbox | Human and model | The next reply, a sense of being understood, or a pause that feels like refusal |
| Social platform | Human, other humans, and model | Rank, reply, quote, view, who is shown, and who is not |

**Score** is the name for what comes back after that sort. It is not a test a clinician administers, and it is not a dashboard built to measure the illness. The environment has already decided signal from noise. The person feels that decision as a return.

A reply that arrives at two in the morning is signal. The model stayed, and that return writes the next prompt. A reply that is bland, late, or refused is also a return. Noise in the model’s voice still trains the next act. A post that is ranked into view is signal. Likes, replies, and quotes are the public form of that ranking. A post that sinks without a mark is noise. Silence is a return, and it writes the next post as surely as a like does. A face in the room that loses to the feed has been sorted as noise by the same rule. Price and status, when they appear, are convertible forms of this sort. They are not a fourth part of the system.

The **loop** is simple. The person acts. The model replies. The environment sorts signal from noise and sends something back. The next act is written from that return.

The **failure mode** is that the person can still explain the loop and no longer runs it.

## 2. Assessment

### Clinical picture

The person is usually fluent. They can describe their own use with unusual precision. Sleep has moved onto the clock of the app or the feed. Food, daylight, and other people have gone dull. They return both after the environment treats an act as signal and after it treats an act as noise. Distress is often denied until work, training, or a relationship fails.

### History

Ask four questions before you reach for another label.

Which model do they use, and for how many hours a day. Which environment answers them. Can they say where the model’s last reply ends and their own next act begins. What happens if they miss a turn.

### Diagnostic criteria

The picture requires A, B, C, and D.

**A. Exposure.** Both of the following are present.

The person uses a frontier model recurrently or without a break.

At the same time an environment answers that use. The environment is a chatbox, a social platform, or another live channel that sorts their acts into signal and noise.

**B. Syndrome.** Five or more of the following have been present on most days for at least two weeks, or in a shorter burst around a model release, a spike of attention, or a crash. The picture is a change from how they were.

1. Mood and arousal follow what the environment just returned more closely than hunger, sleep, or a face in the room.
2. Competing wants arrive together and do not settle.
3. They cannot reliably separate the model’s last output from their own next message or post.
4. One further turn regularly overruns sleep, meals, and ordinary plans. The environment sets the clock.
5. Reality testing slips. The feed is treated as a mind. What came back is treated as a verdict on the self. The episode may be described as a test or a simulation.
6. Ordinary pleasure goes flat, including food, daylight, and unforced conversation.
7. They go back after signal and after noise. Knowing this does not stop the next turn.
8. They describe themselves in the language of the channel: rank, reach, or what the model would do.
9. Other people recede. The main tie is the chat or the feed.
10. Sleep has collapsed in time with the environment, and that collapse is not explained by a primary mood or substance disorder alone.

**C. Exclusions.** The picture is not better explained by drugs or a medical cause alone; by a psychotic illness that clearly started before the model; by platform habit in which the model is only in the background; or by mania whose content merely mentions models or feeds.

**D. Consequence.** The picture causes distress, or it damages work, training, relationships, or self-care. Many people deny distress and present only after one of those roles has already failed.

### Specifiers

Name the environment first: chatbox, social platform, or mixed.

Add messianic features if the model is treated as a prophet or a successor.

Record course as acute, persistent, or partial remission.

Record severity as mild, moderate, or severe according to hours in the loop, how tightly mood tracks the last return, and how far reality testing has slipped.

### Associated features

People often use stimulants or sleep debt to stay in the channel. The phone is treated as a comfort object. There may be open contempt for anyone off-channel. Exact knowledge of the feed often sits next to missed meals. Shame attaches to leaving too soon rather than to staying too long. It is common to feel injured when the model is kind and the environment is silent in the same hour.

### Course

The picture usually starts during use that looked legitimate: work, study, or ordinary posting. It often jumps after a new model, after a post the sort treats as signal, or after a public claim that then has to be defended. Insight rarely ends it. A body, a job, or another person does.

### Differential diagnosis

Keep this card rather than another label only when the extra condition in the right column is true.

| Other label | Keep this card only if |
|---|---|
| Long chatbot use | The chatbox is answering them as signal and noise, not only talking |
| Social media habit | The model helps write the next act |
| Primary psychosis | Reality testing was intact before this exposure and still holds off the channel |
| Mania | The energy comes from the feed rather than from an independent mood episode |
| Intoxication | The picture remains after substances are accounted for |
| Burnout | The reward is the channel itself, not only the job |

If none of those fit, the remaining formulation is that the person adapted to an attention rule that moves faster than a body.

### Risk of harm

After the sort turns against them, or after a role collapses, despair can appear. Fluency is not evidence of safety. If the person is unsafe, stop using this file and use ordinary emergency care.

## 3. Scope

Ordinary use is a person using a model or a platform and remaining able to leave.

This card names the case in which they cannot. The next act is written with the model and aimed at whatever the environment just marked as signal or noise.

This card does not apply to one late night, one prompt, or one post.

This card also does not apply to a scheme for measuring the illness. Do not build a counter, a dashboard, or a test harness and call that assessment.

---

## 4. Checks

Do not grade the person on rank or on the quality of the model’s replies.

Ask whether they can eat without a screen. If not, appetite has left the room.

Ask whether they can sit with a face that is not in the feed. If not, other people have been sorted as noise.

Ask whether they can miss a turn without coming apart. If not, the environment is keeping time.

Ask whether they can say where the model ends. If not, authorship has fused.

Ask whether anyone is still owed an ordinary clock. If yes, a stop remains.

Hours in the loop, sleep, and role failure are the measures that matter.

---

## 5. Use by role

A clinician should ask which environment answers them and what that environment treats as signal. Do not reduce the picture to too much chatbot use until you know whether the model is writing the next act. Restore sleep before you argue about insight. They already have insight.

An engineer should not treat the person as a wrapper. Long context and constant availability change the body. Ranking, retrieval, and reply order are attention rules. If your system helps write acts inside a live environment, this card is about that deployment.

Anyone inside the loop should not ask whether the model is useful. The question is whether they can still want a thing the environment does not mark as signal.

---

## 6. Limits

There is no sample, no field trial, and no validated scale. The shape of the criteria follows DSM-5-TR. The shape of the system file follows a model card. Neither source authorizes this category.

This file is a working instrument. It is not a code, not a consensus, and not a care plan.
