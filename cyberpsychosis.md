## 1. System

The system has three parts.

The **human** is the person in the room. They have a body, a need for sleep, and a life from which they can leave the channel.

The **model** is a frontier generative system. It remains available without a natural stop.

The **environment** is the channel that answers that pair in real time. It is not the internet as a whole. It is a channel that already contains an attention rule. Some events are treated as signal and sent back. The rest is treated as noise and dropped. That rule belongs to the environment. This card does not ask anyone to build a new one.

| Environment | Parties | What the attention rule returns |
|---|---|---|
| Chatbox | Human and model | The next reply, a sense of being understood, or a pause that feels like refusal |
| Social platform | Human, other humans, and model | Rank, reply, quote, view, who is shown, and who is not |

**Score** is the name for what comes back after that sort. It is not a test a clinician administers. It is not a dashboard built to measure the illness. The environment has already decided signal from noise. The person feels that decision as a return.

A reply that arrives at two in the morning is signal. The model stayed, and that return writes the next prompt. A reply that is bland, late, or refused is also a return. Noise in the model’s voice still shapes the next act. A post that is ranked into view is signal. Likes, replies, and quotes are the public form of that ranking. A post that sinks without a mark is noise. Silence is a return, and it writes the next post as surely as a like does. A face in the room that loses to the feed has been sorted as noise by the same rule. Price and status, when they appear, are convertible forms of this sort. They are not a fourth part of the system.

The **loop** is as follows. The person acts. The model replies. The environment sorts signal from noise and sends something back. The next act is written from that return.

The **failure mode** is loss of voluntary control over the loop. The person can still describe what is happening. Description does not restore control. Capability of the model is not a measure of severity.

## 2. Assessment

### Diagnostic features

The person is usually fluent and can describe their own use with unusual precision. Sleep has moved onto the clock of the app or the feed. Food, daylight, and other people have become less rewarding. They resume use both after the environment treats an act as signal and after it treats an act as noise. Distress is often denied until work, training, or a relationship fails.

Internet-native speech, technical fluency, and dark humour are not symptoms. They become relevant only when they replace unmediated relatedness and the person can no longer leave them.

### History

Ask four questions before you apply another label. Which model do they use, and for how many hours each day? Which environment answers them, and what does that environment treat as signal? Can they say where the model’s last reply ends and their own next act begins? What happens if they miss a turn?

### Diagnostic criteria

The diagnosis, as a provisional working category, requires A, B, C, and D.

**A. Exposure.** Both of the following are present.

The person has engaged in recurrent or continuous use of a frontier generative model.

At the same time, an environment has been answering that use. The environment is a chatbox, a social platform, or another live channel that sorts the person’s acts into signal and noise.

**B. Syndrome.** Five or more of the following have been present on most days during a two-week period, or for a shorter period if onset is tightly linked to a model release, a sudden spike of attention, or an abrupt collapse of attention. The symptoms represent a change from previous functioning.

1. Mood and arousal track what the environment just returned more closely than hunger, sleep, or the presence of another person.
2. Competing motivational states occur together and do not resolve. These commonly include fear of missing the next return, attachment to the model, and contempt for any pace the environment does not reward.
3. The person cannot reliably distinguish the model’s last output from their own next message or post.
4. Further turns repeatedly overrun sleep, meals, and planned activity. The environment has become the timekeeper.
5. Reality testing is impaired. The person treats the feed as if it were a mind, treats what came back as a verdict on the self, or holds that the episode is a test or a simulation.
6. There is markedly reduced pleasure in ordinary unmediated activity, including food, daylight, and conversation that is not in the channel.
7. The person resumes use after both signal and noise. Knowledge of this pattern does not prevent the next turn.
8. Self-description has shifted into the language of the channel, including rank, reach, or what the model would do.
9. Other relationships recede. The primary tie is the chat or the feed.
10. Sleep is disrupted in phase with the environment, and that disruption is not accounted for by a primary mood or substance disorder alone.

**C. Exclusions.** The disturbance is not attributable to the physiological effects of a substance or another medical condition as the sole cause. The picture is not better explained by a psychotic disorder that clearly began before frontier-model exposure; by platform habit in which the model is only incidental; or by a manic episode whose content only happens to mention models or feeds.

**D. Consequence.** The symptoms cause clinically significant distress, or they impair social, occupational, or other important functioning, including self-care. A substantial fraction of people deny distress and present only after one of those roles has already failed.

### Specifiers

Record the environment first: chatbox, social platform, or mixed.

Specify **with messianic features** if the model is treated as a prophet, parent, or successor.

Specify course as **acute** if onset or marked worsening occurs within days of a model release or a sudden change in what the environment treats as signal; as **persistent**; or as **in partial remission** if an attempt to leave has been made and residual coupling remains.

Specify current severity as mild, moderate, or severe using hours in the loop, how tightly mood tracks the last return, and the degree of reality-testing failure.

### Associated features supporting diagnosis

Stimulant use, high-dose caffeine, nicotine, or intentional sleep debt are common and are used to remain in the channel. The phone or terminal is treated as a comfort object. Contempt for people outside the channel may be open. Detailed knowledge of ranking and reply patterns often coexists with neglect of meals. Shame attaches to leaving too early rather than to remaining too long. Many people describe a particular injury when the model is kind and the environment is silent in the same hour.

### Development and course

Onset is usually insidious during use that appeared legitimate, including work, study, or ordinary posting. A discrete worsening often follows a new model, a post that the attention rule treats as signal, or a public claim that then has to be defended. Voluntary remission driven by insight alone is uncommon. Exit, when it occurs, is usually forced by the body, by an occupational demand, or by another person who will not be sorted as noise.

### Differential diagnosis

Apply this working category rather than the label on the left only when the condition on the right is met.

| Other label | Prefer this card only if |
|---|---|
| Prolonged chatbot use | The chatbox is returning signal and noise that shape the next act, not only conversation |
| Social media habit | The model co-authors the next act |
| Primary psychotic disorder | Reality testing was intact before this exposure and remains largely intact off the channel |
| Manic episode | Elevated energy is supplied by the feed rather than by an independent mood episode |
| Substance intoxication or withdrawal | The picture persists after substances are accounted for |
| Occupational burnout | The reward is the channel itself, not only the job |

If none of those labels fit, the remaining formulation is that the person has adapted to an attention rule that updates faster than a body can.

### Comorbidity

Common companions include stimulant use, delayed sleep-wake phase, social withdrawal, and a depressive turn after the attention rule stops treating the person as signal. Ritualized checking of the channel is common and can resemble an obsessive-compulsive picture without meeting that disorder on its own.

### Risk of harm

Despair may appear after the attention rule turns against the person, or after a role collapses. Fluent self-observation is not protective. If the person is at risk of harm, stop using this file and follow ordinary emergency pathways.

## 3. Scope

Ordinary use is a person using a model or a platform and remaining able to leave.

This card names the case in which they cannot. The next act is written with the model and aimed at whatever the environment just marked as signal or noise.

This card does not apply to a single late night, a single prompt, or a single post.

This card does not authorize a scheme for measuring the illness. Do not build a counter, a dashboard, or a test harness and call that assessment. Model quality is not a severity score.

---

## 4. Checks

Do not grade the person on rank or on the quality of the model’s replies.

Ask whether they can eat without a screen. If they cannot, appetite has left the room.

Ask whether they can sit with a person who is not in the feed. If they cannot, other people have been sorted as noise.

Ask whether they can miss a turn without marked distress or immediate return. If they cannot, the environment is keeping time.

Ask whether they can say where the model’s output ends and their own act begins. If they cannot, authorship has fused.

Ask whether any unmediated obligation still has a clock they cannot postpone. If it does, a stop remains.

Hours in the loop, sleep, and role failure are the measures that matter.

---

## 5. Use

Establish which environment answers the person and what that environment treats as signal. Do not reduce the picture to excessive chatbot use until it is clear whether the model is writing the next act. Restore sleep and an ordinary clock before debating insight. Insight is usually already present and has not ended the loop.

Do not treat the human as a wrapper around the model. Long context and constant availability change sleep and control. Ranking, retrieval, and reply order are attention rules already in the environment. If a system co-authors acts inside such an environment, this card is about that deployment. Do not add an evaluation harness that scores the person.

The first question is not whether the model is useful. The relevant question is whether the person can still want something the environment does not mark as signal.

---

## 6. Limits

There is no epidemiological sample, no field trial, and no validated severity scale. The shape of the criteria follows DSM-5-TR. The shape of the system description follows a model card. Neither source authorizes this category.

This file is a working instrument. It is not a diagnostic code, not a consensus statement, and not a care plan.
